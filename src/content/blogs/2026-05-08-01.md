# De-Spotifying

Towards the end of last year, I began my journey of hosting services from my home server. This meant that 
I no longer am subscribed to Spotify, Netflix, Crunchyroll, and other online streaming services. Currently, I am hosting a Navidrome server which stores and streams my music replacing Spotify. I also am using Koito and ListenBrainz to keep track of what I've been listening history. If you want to see what I've been listening to click [here](https://koito.gnawhleinad.dev/).

The immediate pro that comes with self-hosting is that I am no longer paying monthly subscriptions that are progressively getting more and more expensive. Spotify, back in 2023, sold their individual subscription plan at $9.99/mo compared to $12.99/mo as of today ([see all the changes](https://pricetimeline.com/data/price/spotify-premium)). And its not just Spotify, it's almost everything now. Another pro is that I get to keep the media I paid for. Frequently, platforms take down content off their servers due to licensing or other issues, and you'll never know whether your favorite show or artist will be next.


Although, it's nice to own the media I paid for now, it's not without its pain points. It's frickin' **expensive**! I know I just talked about how moving to self-hosting saved me from coughing up money every month to different streaming providers, but it really just moved where I spent money on to another place. First is the investing in computer hardware which at the time of when I bought all the components, the AI datacenters decided that the time for reasonably priced consumer computer parts was over. Second, buying a VPS (virtual private server) to circumnavigate my CG-NAT ISP. Today's internet service providers seldom sell their internet plans to allow their consumers to port-forward anymore. So in order for me to access my music library to host, I buy a VPS replacing a monthly subscription with another subscription. Third, I love to listen to a wide variety of music which translates into having to buy many upon many songs/albums. I do miss the convenience of having practically unlimited music at my fingertips when using Spotify, and it can be argued that I am now being more intentional about what music I choose to listen to, but it raises another issue: music discovery. 

I have to admit, I discovered so many great artists using Spotify's recommendation algorithm. Shoot, some of my favorite artists like Number Girl, itsue, kinokoteikoku, and sooooo many more were found through it. It was also good at recommended new, up-and-coming artists which is something I care about finding. And honestly speaking, I still don't have a good and mature methodology in discovering new music. I am subscribed to some Youtube channels such as [The First Take](https://www.youtube.com/@The_FirstTake), [Audiotree](https://www.youtube.com/@audiotree), and [New Now](https://www.youtube.com/@mother_night) but they don't quite fill Spotify's shoes. I am currently looking at using [ListenBrainz](https://listenbrainz.org/) as a potential method paired with [Explo](https://github.com/LumePart/Explo).

How Explo works is that it uses ListenBrainz's "Weekly Exploration" playlist to download mp3 files into your media server. Obviously, this means that the music being downloaded are NOT being paid for and could be considered _<u>piracy</u>_. But it can be argued that it isn't as the content is published to a public platform where it can be consumed for "free" by anyone. I'm not sure if it sits right with me to do this; it's something I've yet to decide for myself. My buying philosophy is that I'll try my best to buy music from sources that will pay the artist directly. And if there is no other way to digitally or physically obtain it, then perhaps by purchasing the artist's or band's merch can offset the illicit means of obtaining music.

Now, I won't judge anyone that pirate music as there are many nuances and gray areas. But I do find myself being less tolerant of people who pirate while unashamedly touting that piracy is inherently moral no matter what. That is disregarding and disrespecting the effort and passion that artists put forth into creating their art. I have a lot more of thoughts on this but I won't get to deep into it as I'd be digressing.

All that being said, digital ownership is a doozy, self-hosting is expensive, being intentional is hard, but to me it's all worth it.
